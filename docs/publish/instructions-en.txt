AI Passport Mic · Pocket Wireless Microphone
Turn AI Passport into a pocket wireless microphone. In meetings, recordings or livestreams,
the device itself is your mic — no headset cable, no leaning into the laptop.

Step 1 — Download and install the desktop app (do this first)
The firmware only captures and transmits audio. Getting that audio into your meeting app is
done by a companion desktop app. Without it the play will not work.

1. Open this download page on your Mac (macOS only; Intel and Apple silicon both work):
   https://github.com/cnryb/ai-passport-mic/releases/latest
2. Under Assets, pick the zip that matches your Mac:
   - Apple silicon (M1/M2/M3/M4): AI-Passport-Mic-0.1.0-macos-arm64.zip
   - Intel: AI-Passport-Mic-0.1.0-macos-x86_64.zip
   Not sure which one? Apple menu → About This Mac, look at the "Chip" line.
3. Unzip it and drag AI Passport Mic into your Applications folder.
4. The first launch is blocked with "Apple cannot check it for malicious software". That is
   expected: the app is not signed with a paid Apple Developer certificate. Either:
   - Right-click the icon, choose Open, then click Open again. After that a double-click works.
   - Or open Terminal and run:
     xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/AI Passport Mic.app"
5. The app also needs one free virtual audio driver (BlackHole), which is what lets meeting
   apps pick the device up as a microphone. The app checks for it: when it is missing, the Live
   tab shows "One more step: install a virtual audio driver". Tap "How" and it opens the
   download page — install the 2ch version.

Step 2 — Put the play on the device
1. Hold the power button on the left edge for about half a second. The screen lights up.
2. Connect the device to your Mac with a USB-C cable that carries data, then open the app.
3. Open the "Device flashing" tab. The device shows up as a serial port; tap "Check version",
   then "Write firmware". It takes about five seconds — do not unplug during the write. The
   device restarts and starts advertising.
   No serial port listed? The cable or port is the usual cause: try a data cable and plug
   directly into the Mac (no hub or dongle).

Step 3 — Connect and confirm it works
1. Open the "Live" tab, tap "Scan for devices", pick AI Passport Mic and tap "Connect".
2. Approve the Bluetooth prompt the first time. That is a system prompt; the app cannot do it
   for you.
3. Choose your audio driver in the bottom bar and tap "Verify audio driver". It plays a short
   test tone and reads it back from the input side. PASS means the path works.
4. In your meeting app (Zoom, Teams, Meet…), set the microphone to that virtual driver's
   input. Picking the output instead of the input is the single most common mistake here.

Everyday use
- Tap OK once to start talking, tap again to stop. OK is the talk key by default.
- The device screen shows battery, connection state and what each of the three buttons does.
  Hold UP or DOWN to toggle the screen; it dims on its own after 30 seconds to save power.
- UP and DOWN can be remapped to any shortcut (Enter, Esc, F5…) on the "Button mapping" tab —
  handy for raising a hand or switching slides. That feature needs macOS Accessibility
  permission; the tab walks you through granting it.
- Below 20% battery, pressing a button flashes the backlight three times.

Troubleshooting
- The app will not open, or says it is damaged / cannot be verified: use one of the two fixes
  in Step 1 item 4. If it still fails, check that you are on macOS 11 or later.
- Nobody hears you: tap "Verify audio driver" first. "Loopback almost silent" means the driver
  is missing or not passing audio through. "No samples read" means microphone permission was
  denied — open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone and enable the app.
- Device not found: make sure it is powered on (hold the power button for half a second;
  plugging in the cable alone does not boot it) and that the WeChat mini program on your phone
  is not holding the connection.
- To go back to the factory play: scan the QR code on the back of the device, open the device
  page in Chrome on your computer, and follow "Restore default firmware". This works after
  installing any play.

Notes
This play needs the companion desktop app; the firmware only captures and transmits.
Installing it replaces whatever is currently on the device, so make sure you no longer need
the existing content.
