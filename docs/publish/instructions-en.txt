AI Passport Mic · Pocket Wireless Microphone
Turn AI Passport into a pocket wireless microphone. In meetings, recordings or livestreams,
the device itself is your mic — no headset cable, no leaning into the laptop.

Before you start
1. A Mac (macOS 11 or later; Intel and Apple silicon both work).
2. One free virtual audio driver, which is what lets meeting apps pick the device up as a
   microphone. Tap "How" in the app and it opens the download page: install the 2ch version,
   then tap "Rescan".
3. A USB-C cable that carries data (charge-only cables will not work).

First run
1. Hold the power button on the left edge for about half a second. The screen lights up.
2. Connect the device to your Mac with the USB-C cable and open AI Passport Mic.
   - The first launch may say Apple cannot check the app for malicious software. Right-click
     the icon, choose Open, then Open again. After that a normal double-click works.
3. Open the "Device flashing" tab. The device shows up as a serial port once it is plugged in;
   tap "Check version" first, then "Write firmware". It takes about five seconds — do not
   unplug during the write. The device restarts and starts advertising.
4. Open the "Live" tab, tap "Scan for devices", pick AI Passport Mic and tap "Connect".
5. Approve the Bluetooth prompt the first time. That is a system prompt; the app cannot do it
   for you.
6. Choose your audio driver in the bottom bar and tap "Verify audio driver". It plays a short
   test tone into the driver and reads it back from the input side. PASS means the path works.
7. In your meeting app (Zoom, Teams, Meet…), set the microphone to that virtual driver's
   input. Picking the output instead of the input is the single most common mistake here.

Everyday use
- Tap OK once to start talking, tap again to stop. OK is the talk key by default.
- The device screen shows battery, connection state and what each of the three buttons does.
  Hold UP or DOWN to toggle the screen; it dims on its own after 30 seconds to save power.
- UP and DOWN can be remapped to any shortcut (Enter, Esc, F5…) on the "Button mapping" tab —
  handy for raising a hand or switching slides. That feature needs macOS Accessibility
  permission; the tab walks you through granting it.
- Below 20% battery, pressing a button flashes the backlight three times.

Troubleshooting
- Nobody hears you: tap "Verify audio driver" first. "Loopback almost silent" means the driver
  is missing or not passing audio through. "No samples read" means microphone permission was
  denied — open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone and enable the app.
- Device not found: make sure it is powered on (hold the power button for half a second;
  plugging in the cable alone does not boot it) and that the WeChat mini program on your phone
  is not holding the connection.
- To go back to the factory play: scan the QR code on the back of the device, open the
  device page in Chrome on your computer, and follow "Restore default firmware". This works
  after installing any play.

Notes
This play needs the companion desktop app; the firmware only captures and transmits.
Installing it replaces whatever is currently on the device, so make sure you no longer need
the existing content.
